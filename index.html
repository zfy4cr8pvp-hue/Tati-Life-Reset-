Your 7 Modes

Anchor Points

Tap one to see the moves for that mode.

01

Overwhelmed

The list is too long to even look at, so you start nowhere.
›

Brain dump: get everything out of your head onto paper. Don't sort it, just empty it.

›

Priority of one: pick exactly one thing that matters today. The rest waits.

›

Five-minute rule: commit to five minutes on the thing you're avoiding. You can stop after.

›

Visual planning: swap the wall of text for a whiteboard or sticky notes. Shapes, not paragraphs.

02

Time-Blind

"Five more minutes" was forty minutes ago. Time isn't real until it's gone.
›

Visual timer: one you can watch drain, not just numbers counting down.

›

Time blocking: give every task an actual slot on the calendar, not just a line on a list.

›

Buffer time: pad 15 to 30 minutes around anything with a start time. Your estimate is wrong, plan for it.

›

Countdown alarms: set it for when you need to leave, not when the thing starts.

03

Hyperfocus

You look up and three hours are gone. Haven't moved or checked your phone once.
›

Scheduled breaks: set the stop alarm before you start. Hyperfocus won't stop itself.

›

Movement reminders: a nudge every hour to roll around or shift position, so your body isn't frozen the whole time.

›

Timer resets: restart the clock every time you check back in, so you stay aware time's moving.

›

Accountability check-in: text someone "starting X, checking in at 3" so someone else can pull you out.

04

Emotion-Driven

Zero to ten in half a second. The reaction's out before you decided to have it.
›

Nervous system reset: something physical first. Slow breathing, cold water on your wrists, before you try to think straight.

›

Pause before responding: wait ten seconds, even when it feels urgent. It rarely is.

›

Self-validation: "this makes sense" comes before "was this okay." Don't skip to judgment.

›

Emotional processing: write it or say it out loud. Stuffing it just delays when it leaks out.

05

Forgetful

You knew it a second ago. Now it's just gone, like it was never there.
›

External reminders: the second you think of it, it goes in your phone. Your brain isn't the storage system.

›

Visible storage: keys, meds, whatever you always lose, keep it out in the open.

›

Habit stacking: tie it to something automatic. "After I roll into the kitchen, I check my meds."

›

Checklists: anything repeated, write the steps once. Stop rebuilding it every time.

06

Perfectionist

Still planning the plan. It has to be right or it doesn't start at all.
›

Imperfect action: start messy. You can't edit a blank page.

›

Tiny first step: shrink the task until it's almost too easy to say no to.

›

Done over perfect: ask if this actually needs to be perfect, or just needs to exist.

›

Progress tracking: track that you did it, not how well. Volume beats polish here.

07

High-Masking

Everyone thinks you've got it together. You're the only one who knows what it costs.
›

Boundaries: practice the actual words for "no" or "I need more time" before you need them.

›

Asking for support: say the real ask out loud. Don't wait for someone to notice you're drowning.

›

Energy management: budget your energy like money. Know what's expensive for you and plan around it.

›

Self-compassion: hard for you doesn't mean failing. It means your brain runs differently.

This is a living list. Update it as you find what actually works.
